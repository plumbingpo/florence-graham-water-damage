Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. Repair is a different job. Think of it as all the protective work that comes first, aimed at saving what remains sound. For Florence-Graham homes, that means we stop the source, pull the water out, seal off wet areas and get the structure stable. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. In fact there are two separate stages. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurance companies also bill the two as separate line items, which makes it useful to know your current phase.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. Source control, so no new water enters. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Four: stabilize by pulling materials beyond saving, applying antimicrobial to surfaces and setting up air movers plus dehumidifiers. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

How to Tell You Need Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Plumbing repairs are done, but no one has addressed what got wet.
  • Underfoot the carpet is spongy because the padding below holds water.
  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • A roof or window opening is letting rain into the building.
  • Along the floor, water is travelling out of one room and into another.
  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • Your insurer has told you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.

Why Hire a Professional for Water Damage Mitigation?

Mitigation is a series of judgment calls made quickly. Can this section of drywall dry in place, or does it need cutting out? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Is removing the cabinet toe kick necessary? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Owners are required by most policies to take reasonable steps against further damage, and the insurer will ask for evidence. We photograph conditions on arrival, record moisture readings daily and keep a list of anything removed. That file shows what the water did and what was done about it. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Braided steel supply lines should take the place of rubber ones on the washing machine about every five years.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Regrade low spots beside the house where water gathers after rain.
  • Install an automatic shutoff valve that closes the main when a leak is detected.
  • Have your water heater inspected yearly and replace it before the tank fails.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
